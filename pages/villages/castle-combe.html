A SUMMARY

Castle Combe is a perfectly preserved Wiltshire village where honey-coloured stone cottages gather around a medieval market cross.

Tucked into a wooded valley north of Bath, Castle Combe has served as a backdrop for period dramas and remains one of the most photographed villages in England. Its narrow lanes, stone bridge, and 13th-century church create an atmosphere of timeless stillness.

The village grew around a Norman castle long since vanished, but the name endures. The Market Cross and St Andrew's Church anchor the centre, while the By Brook winds past weavers' cottages that recall the village's textile heritage.

Despite its cinematic fame, Castle Combe retains a hushed, residential character. Visitors are asked to respect private property and park in the designated car park at the top of the hill before walking down into the village.

Early morning and late afternoon offer the best light on the limestone facades, when tour groups have thinned and the valley feels almost exclusively yours.

ESSENTIAL EXPERIENCES

01

The Market Cross

A 14th-century stone cross marks the heart of the village and frames the classic view down the main street.

02

St Andrew's Church

Notable for its faceless clock—one of the oldest working clocks in the country—and fine medieval stonework.

03

The Bridge

The low stone bridge over the By Brook is the village's most iconic vantage point for photography.

GETTING HERE

train

BY RAIL

The nearest station is Chippenham (5 miles). Services from London Paddington and Bristol. Taxis to the village should be booked in advance.

directions_car

PARKING

Use the main visitor car park at the top of the hill—on-street parking in the village is extremely limited and discouraged.
